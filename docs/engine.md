# Engine design

Decided 2026-11-09, the first night. Each choice gives its reason so it can be argued with. Evidence: [research/2026-11-09-engine-prior-art.md](research/2026-11-09-engine-prior-art.md).

## The choice: Bevy for the frame, our own crates for the world

**Bevy 0.20, pinned, for windowing, rendering, assets, glTF, skinned animation, input, UI and its ECS. Our own code for everything that is the game: voxels, meshing, simulation, creatures' behaviour.**

Why not our own engine on wgpu:

- We are one person and an agent, and not game developers. A renderer, an asset system, skinned animation with blending and a glTF loader are months of work that Bevy has, and none of it is what makes this game different.
- Every maintained Rust voxel library found targets Bevy. The Rust voxel games that wrote their own renderer (Veloren, Tiny Glade) had rendering specialists or years of volunteers.
- The thing we had to prove first, generated models that animate, needed exactly the parts Bevy ships. It took one evening, not a quarter.

What Bevy costs, and how we contain it:

- **API churn**: a release every three to five months, each breaking. We pin the version, upgrade deliberately at most once a release, and agents read the local examples instead of trusting memory ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), Rules).
- **Lock-in**: kept shallow. Voxel data and meshing (`cube_voxel`) use no Bevy types, and the simulation will not either. If Bevy's renderer ever becomes the limit for terrain, we replace how chunks are drawn (a custom material or render pass) and keep the rest. Tiny Glade did exactly that: Bevy's ECS and app, its own renderer.
- **Custom shaders** break on upgrades (0.20 moved WGSL to WESL). So shaders are for environment effects only, written late, kept few.

## Crates

| Crate | Depends on | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `cube_voxel` | glam | Blocks, chunks (32³), the world map, meshing to plain vertex arrays |
| `cube_sim` | cube_voxel, serde | The simulation at a fixed 60 ticks a second, runnable and testable with no window: making the world from a seed (`terrain`), measuring it (`survey`), how a body moves through voxels (`body`), the creatures and one tick of all of them (`life`), what each decides (`mind`), seeded randomness (`rng`). To come: block changes, needs, saving |
| `cube_game` | bevy, cube_sim, cube_voxel | Drawing and input: it draws what the simulation holds and feeds the player's keys in |
| `world` | cube_sim, image | `cargo world`: the world and its creatures judged with no window, in numbers and one map |
| `forge` | glam, serde, image | The creature pipeline; never linked into the game |

The rule this protects: **the simulation never needs a window.** A big sandbox with heavy simulation will be server-authoritative sooner or later, and a simulation that runs headless is also one an agent can test in milliseconds.

## Voxels

- **A cell is 0.25 m**, a chunk 32³ cells (8 m): a quarter of Minecraft's 1 m, so a hillside reads as a slope and a tree's crown as round, and a 1.75 m player is seven cells tall. Chosen 2026-11-10 from a side-by-side picture against 0.5 m, before block editing exists, when it was cheapest to change. It costs 64 cells a cubic metre against Minecraft's one: the island is 1,500 chunks and 2.1 million faces, about 900 MB at its peak while starting. So storage and meshing are what to watch (palette-compress chunks, mesh greedily), and digging and building will want to work in pieces bigger than a cell. Everything that means a distance (terrain shapes, tree sizes, how high a body steps) is written in metres and turned to cells with `CELL`, so it can change again.
- **Any cell can break into 5 cm bits** (`SPLIT` 5, so 125 to a cell), and only broken cells cost more. Tried 2026-11-10 to answer whether a fine grid could be the rule without the whole world being stored that fine: it can. A cell is whole until something smaller than a cell happens to it (a hole dug in it, something built on it), then it holds its own 125 bits; left all one thing again, it mends. Bodies collide with bits, rays stop at them, and a hole is a rounded bowl, not a missing cube. So 5 cm is the smallest thing in the world and the unit things are placed on, while hills are stored a hundred and twenty-five times cheaper. Measured (`cargo world dig`): a hole 0.3 to 1.6 m across breaks about 35 cells and costs 5 kB; 120 of them add 7% to the faces drawn; a changed chunk is drawn again in under 2 ms; creatures live among them without ending up in the ground. The cost that grows is faces: a broken cell's flat side is 25 little squares where a whole cell's is one, so 5,000 holes on this island triple what is drawn. Merging flat faces (greedy meshing) is the answer and is not built.
- **Meshing today**: one quad per visible face, ambient occlusion baked into vertex colour, slight per-cell tint. No textures. About the simplest thing that does not look like one flat tile.
- **The Eco look is shaped blocks, not smooth isosurfaces**: blocks keep the grid (so building and simulation stay simple) but draw as mesh pieces chosen by their neighbours (bevelled edges, slopes, rounded corners). That is the main lever for "much better than Minecraft" and the next terrain job. Marching cubes or surface nets would look smoother but lose the block grid that building and simulation want.
- **Deep water is harder to see through**: a water face's alpha grows with the water under it, so a ford shows its bed and the sea does not. The sea beyond the island is four plain sheets out past the fog.
- **Not yet**: greedy meshing (measured elsewhere at 50 to 350 µs a chunk for plain meshing, so not urgent), meshing off the main thread, level of detail, chunk streaming, saving.

### What grows is drawn as shapes

- **`plants` says what shape it grew** (`plants::recorded`, `Form`): as it sets the cells of a trunk, a limb or a mass of leaf, it notes the limb (from, to, how thick at each end, what bark) or the clump (middle, reach, what leaf). The game records this as it makes each region (`world::regions`), keeps the shapes whose middle lies in the region (`flora::Forms`), and makes one mesh a region from them (`flora::grow`): a seven-sided tapering tube a limb, and for a clump some tens to two hundred small cards over an ellipsoid, each a piece of the leaf picture with its gaps, lit as the mass and not as a card, free to the wind.
- **The mesher does not draw wood or leaf cells** when that is on (`mesh::MODELLED`), nor let them hide the ground under them. They are still cells: bodies stop at trunks, creatures do not climb crowns.
- **Cost**: frames went from 11 ms to 8 in the meadow; cube crowns were more faces than the cards that replaced them.
- **Smooth ground is one surface** (`mesh::Heights`): where each column's ground ends is found from the cells, the surface at a corner is the mean of the sixteen columns round it, and a quad is drawn a column with its corners on that surface and its colour the mean of what tops those columns; steep, it shows earth and then stone. No risers are drawn at all. The first smooth ground eased each cube's own corners, which left a stretched riser on every steep face (sawteeth up a cliff) and a zigzag wherever two materials met. The far levels draw the same surface a quad to two or four columns.
- **The ground was smooth by default for a few hours** (`Ground::Smooth`, now `--smooth`; the default is blocks again, see below), now that nothing natural beside it is cubes; and the water's surface runs two cells on under the bank, so a shoreline is where smooth ground meets it and not a sawn edge.
- **What grows is cubes by default** (`flora::Shape::limb_cubes`, `clump_cubes`, `stone_cubes`, 2026-11-11; `--shapes` for the low-poly forms). Made from the same `plants::Form`, at three levels:
  - Near (a region within 5 m): trunks and heavy limbs in cubes of 10 cm, round to the cube, swelling into stepped roots; a lesser limb is one line of cubes as thick as it is (a hundred pine boughs drawn round were more faces than the rest of the wood, unseen under their needles). Each cube its own shade of bark and a line of them along the grain shares one, so furrows; moss on roots and foot; cut wood on an end nothing grows from. Leaf is boxes from a hand to 60 cm, each its own green, lit on top and dark under, over a dark heart. Stone is cubes in tipped beds of differing shade, moss and lichen on top.
  - To 46 m: the world's 25 cm cells for wood thicker than 20 cm, larger and fewer leaf boxes, a conifer's spray one plate.
  - Beyond: 50 cm, the largest masses only, and no shadow cast.
  - Cost: the first try was 4.2 million faces in a forest view and 27 ms. With the levels above it is 1.7 million and 16 ms; meadow 13 ms. A flora mesh carries `world::Faces`, so a picture's `pace` counts it.
- **So do the ground's blocks** (`mesh_chunk`, the stepped path): a top is darkened at a corner by how many of the three columns round it stand over it by a hand or more, so the foot of every bank and terrace is in shade.
- **Cubes shade their own corners** (`flora::Shape::cubes`): a corner of a face is darkened by how many cubes stand over it beside that face, the occlusion every good cube renderer has (research, 1). Baked into the corner's colour, so it costs nothing to draw and reaches farther than screen-space occlusion's few texels.
- **A limb has one shape, and it is the simulation's** (`plants::Limb::fill`, 2026-11-11): round, tapering, wandering, ridged, a rooted trunk swelling into buttresses, in cubes of any size on the world's grid. The simulation fills cells from it (25 cm) and the game draws it finer (10 and 15 cm), so what stops a body is what is seen, to a cell. Before, cells were a plain cone with root sticks and the picture had the swell and the bends: the developer walked into wood he could see and was stopped by wood he could not. Not yet to the bit: a body is stopped by the cell, the eye sees the 10 cm cube.
- **Leaf does not stop a body** (`body`): a person pushes through a crown or a bush and is stopped by wood.
- **A leaf mass is a lump with its boxes on it** (`flora::Shape::clump_cubes`): a body of four crossed boxes, as near an egg as boxes come, and the smaller boxes sitting on its surface. And every limb that carries leaf is drawn at every distance, as one line of cubes if need be (only a conifer's drooping boughs, hidden under their needles, are dropped). The developer's pictures showed why: boxes hung in the air round a slab, and a young tree by the river was a crown with no tree under it.
- **Seen farther** (the developer: "draw distance is really poor"): real ground and trees are made to 208 m (176), the mist is half as thick, and there is a fourth level of detail for both (ground in 2 m blocks past 168 m; a tree past 104 m is its trunk and one box to a mass). Measured on a quiet machine it was 19 ms in a wood, and the range was not the cost (176 m measured the same). `FLORA_TRACE=1` prints each region's faces by level and part as it is made, and added up it was wood, not leaf: an oak's great limbs, a metre through, in 10 cm cubes high overhead (half a million faces in the one nearest region), and far trunks' stair-stepped cubes (half a million over the view). So wood is cut fine only within reach of the ground (4.5 m near, 6 m in the next ring) and in cubes twice the size above, and a far trunk is one box. Meadow 13 ms, wood 15, river 16, from the air 12. Two guesses made before measuring (leaf boxes, then mid-range limbs) changed nothing.
- **Cube trees and rocks are drawn with the whole paint** (`Look::whole`: nothing cut out, outsides only). The cut-out material the ground cover needs is far dearer where large faces lie over one another, as a crown's boxes do: the same forest view was 19 ms with it and 17 without, at the same faces. With the trees raised (`scale`: oak to 30 m, pine to 46, giants a quarter over) a great trunk is cubes of 15 cm and leaf boxes are capped by level; forest 17 ms, meadow 13.
- **Grass is slats; ferns and flowers are bits** (`mesh::blade`; `--bits` for grass as columns of bits, `--blades` for the pointed planes). A slat is a blade in the world's own shapes: square-cut, upright, lying along the grid one way or the other, a bit wide for most of its height and half that above, so a stepped outline and no point. Two faces. Columns of bits were tried first (five faces each, so half as many of them): true to the style, chunky underfoot, and the developer's word was that grass "can still be done efficiently, it just needs to use the right art style". A fern is a short crown and arcs of bits stepping out, up and down; a flower one bit of colour on a stem.
- **The far stand-ins are boxes too** (`far::Shape::crown`, `cone`): three boxes to a broadleaf, four square tiers to a conifer.
- **Limbs are wood, not pipes** (`flora::Shape::limb`): rings along their length that wander a little, ridged along the grain, an end rounded over where another limb goes on and cut wood (end grain) where none does. `plants::bole` notes how many roots a trunk has and the trunk is drawn swelling into them as one piece.
- **Bark is its true size along a limb** (`flora::Shape::limb`): each stretch between two rings takes its own piece of the tile, every ring but the ends laid twice. One tile pulled the length of a pine was bark drawn out five times too long, which read as a plain brown pole.
- **Short grass over a meadow is drawn, not held** (`mesh_chunk`, near level only): most cells of grass with nothing taller on them carry four to six short blades, thick in some patches and thin in others, tinted dry or lush as the ground under them is. The ground was a painted floor with tufts stood on it. A quarter of a million more faces in a meadow view; the frame stayed 13 ms.
- **Three levels of detail for what grows** (`flora::grow`, by a region's distance: 36 m, 88 m), as the ground has: sprigs, cards, a few broad cards. Sprigs everywhere cost 4 ms a frame; with the levels a meadow frame is 13 ms with the voxel ground.
- **Not done**: beyond the made regions the far land still stands rounded stand-ins for trees; rocks, flowers and mushrooms are still little cubes.

### The ground's blocks

- **The land is drawn in blocks of the size its slope calls for** (`mesh::Eased::step`, 2026-11-11; `mesh::Ground::Stepped`, the default). Nearly level ground is cut in steps of a bit (5 cm), so a meadow rolls; a slope is whole cells; steep ground is blocks half a metre a side and a rock face blocks of a metre. The land gets blockier as it gets wilder, and no two hillsides are the same staircase, which is what every slope was when each was drawn as its 0.25 m cubes. It is voxels throughout: only the size varies.
- **The cells are untouched.** Digging, building and what a body stands on are the same 0.25 m cells and bits; this is how the top cell of each column of land (`Block::ground`: soil, sand, snow, stone) is drawn. Its height is the mean height of the ground in the sixteen columns round it, rounded to the block's size, with a riser down to each neighbour that stands lower. Ground cover, the bits of a broken cell and a creature's feet (`mesh::eased_under`) are drawn at that height.
- **Stone that is not land is drawn as its cubes**: a column whose top would be drawn more than a few cells from where it is (a block standing alone, something built) falls back to plain cubes.
- **A low riser is lit and coloured as the ground it is a fold of.** Lit as a wall it took the warm light of the horizon while the ground beside it took the blue of the sky overhead, and a forest floor was a grid of orange lines.
- **Tried and set aside: the ground eased fully smooth** (`--smooth`). It was built first, from a misreading of what was asked. Smooth land under cube trees was two games in one picture. `--cubes` draws the old plain cubes.
- **Block size goes by place as well as slope** (`mesh::rough`): the land has rough country some twenty metres over, where a modest slope is already half-metre and metre blocks, and smooth country cut fine. By slope alone everything in one view was one size, and the developer saw only uniform voxels.
- **The far levels of detail draw the same ground** (`Eased::far_top`): each group of cells takes the eased height at its middle, in blocks its own size where the land is steep. Drawn as plain cubes of their own size the far levels were a different hill, striped in half-metre stairs from 40 m out.
- **Not done**: a cube-drawn neighbour of a stepped column can leave a gap; thin gaps show where two levels of detail meet on steep rock.

- **Blocks are the default again** (2026-11-11, the developer's word: voxels were never the trouble). `cargo world survey` counts faces as the game draws them, trees as shapes.

### Light

- **The sky is air scattering the sun** (Bevy's `Atmosphere`, 2026-11-11), so the colour of the sunlight, of the sky and of the light that fills shadows all come from where the sun is, and will follow it when there is a time of day. Before, a white sun and a flat blue fill lit everything evenly and nothing had depth.
- **The sun stands at 35 degrees**: everything throws a shadow longer than itself.
- **On every camera that looks at the world** (`world::look`): exposure for full sun, a filmic roll-off (TonyMcMapface), a little more saturation and contrast, bloom, screen-space ambient occlusion for the depth between one thing and another, and haze that warms toward the sun.
- **Tried and backed out the same hour**: lit air (volumetric fog) over the whole island turned the sky purple and the grass yellow; a stronger grade made shade black. Light shafts want a volume placed where they are wanted (a wood, a morning), not one over everything.
- **The calendar is the simulation's** (`cube_sim::sky`, 2026-11-11): one number, `when`, days since the world began with the hour as its fraction. A day is 24 minutes (a minute to the hour), seven days a season, four seasons a year from spring; the world starts mid-spring at 15:00. The sun rises in the east, stands to the south at noon, and swings with the year: summer noons 81 degrees up and days 13.5 hours, winter 43 degrees and 10.5. The moon goes through its phases once a season: it trails the sun by as much as it is through them, so a full moon rises at sunset and a new moon is not in the night sky at all; `moonlight` is how full it is (steeply: a half moon gives a third of a full one) and nothing when it is down. `--day N --hour H` on any scene.
- **The moon is drawn as what it is** (`air::heavens`): a ball with one bright half, kept turned to the sun, so its phase is seen by the real geometry. `--view moon --day N --hour H` looks straight at it. (The first try slid a dark disc over a bright one; a close picture showed a black ball beside the moon.)
- **Seasons are drawn by the wind shader** (`world::season_look`, a uniform on the land's material): leaves and grass take the season's tint by how much of a leaf each corner is (the number that already says how free it is to move), and in winter snow lies on whatever faces up. No block changes: the simulation's world is the same in every season. Not done: bare trees in winter, ice, anything living that notices the season.
- **The sky is drawn from the hour** (`world::sky`): the sun goes round and under and is put out below the horizon (the air does the reddening and dimming above it); a moon comes up opposite, bluer and brighter than a real one; the camera's exposure follows the sun's height, opening fourteen stops by night as an eye does, because a night as dark as night cannot be played in; colour drains under the moon; the haze warms at a low sun and nearly goes at night. The exposure for each height was set by eye against pictures (`--hour H` on any scene).
- **Mood is made, not found** (2026-11-11, after the first day and night were correct and flat):
  - *Cloud shadows.* A tile of soft patches made in code is laid in the sun's light (`DirectionalLightTexture`, Bevy's `pbr_light_textures` feature) and drifted downwind, so hillsides go dim and bright again. The light's own size sets the tile's (420 m); shadow cascades use only its rotation, so they are not disturbed.
  - *Mist.* A layer of thin lit air 24 m deep lies over the low ground (`FogVolume`, `VolumetricFog` on the camera, `VolumetricLight` on the sun): shafts of sun between trunks, far woods in haze. Kept low so the sky stays clear, and thinned and tinted with the sun as it drops, or a low sun floods it and whites the picture out. (Over the whole island and untinted it turned the sky purple.)
  - *A low sun is made stronger and amber.* True to life the last hour of sun is weak and the sky's flat fill takes the picture; the gold of evening is wanted more than the truth. Exposure near the horizon is held down so the sky keeps its colour.
  - *Night has a sky.* Stars on a dome round the camera, a moon where its light comes from, and one mote in six a firefly that swells and fades.
- **Mountains stand to the north** (`terrain::column`). The sun goes round by the south, so a mountain there is lit all day and never lies between the sun and the lowland. Placed anywhere, seed 6 had it due west and the start was in its shadow from mid afternoon: an hour was lost looking for a fault in the cloud shadows before a picture at another hour showed it.
- **Not done**: clouds to look at, weather, creatures that keep hours, and torches that can be placed.
- **The torch** (`player::torch`, 2026-11-11): a warm point light at the controlled creature's shoulder, with shadows and a waver; T lights it and puts it out, `--torch` starts with it lit. It costs about 4 ms a frame here (a shadowed point light draws the land six more times); not yet looked into.

### Wind and the air

- **The season is applied in both wind shaders** (`wind.wesl`, `wind_prepass.wesl`): the second is also what the deferred path draws colour with, and for a day after water began to reflect the land (deferred by default) it left the season out, so autumn and winter were summer and no check knew. `make seasons` (in `make prove`) pictures the meadow in summer and winter and judges the ground's colour: green, then no greener than blue.

- **Pollen thins to nothing within three metres of the camera** (`air::motes`): a glowing speck a hand from the lens bloomed into a white ball the size of a head in every daytime picture.

- **What grows moves in the wind** (2026-11-11). The mesher says how free each corner is to move, in the alpha of its colour: ground and wood are held, a leaf gives two fifths, a blade of grass is held at its foot and free at its tip. Two vertex shaders push each corner downwind by that much (`assets/shaders/wind.wesl` for the picture, `wind_prepass.wesl` for depth and shadows, so a leaf's shadow sways with it): long gusts rolling downwind with a quick flutter under them. The push depends only on where a corner is and the time, so corners that meet move together and nothing tears. The land's material is Bevy's own with those two shaders swapped in (`world::Windy`); nothing else about how it is lit changes.
- **The two shaders hold the same push written twice.** The prepass keeps the time in a different binding from the forward pass, and a shared file imported by both was not tried. Change both together.
- **Things in the air are dressing, not creatures** (`cube_game::air`): pollen that drifts on the same wind and flares crossing a shaft of sun, leaves coming down in the woods, butterflies over the meadows, birds wheeling in flocks high up. They are kept in a box round the camera, the simulation does not know of them, and nothing reacts to them; a bird that can be hunted is a creature and belongs to `cube_sim`. About 330 small meshes in all.
- **Enough creatures to meet** (`Sim::populate`): groups are by the island's area (280 creatures on this one, where there were 145), and what does no harm may live 22 m from the start, hunters 45 m, the hostile 70 m. At 60 m for all but the timid, a player at the start saw a rabbit in the grass and nothing else.
- **Far creatures are neither drawn nor animated** (`motion::far_off`, beyond 120 m), and only near ones have their feet set to the drawn ground. The simulation moves them all the same. With all 280 posed and skinned every frame the frame went from 17 ms to 23; found by switching one thing off at a time (creatures, foliage culling, foliage gaps), not by guessing: the suspects had been the foliage and the torch.
- **Small life has bodies and wings, not boxes** (2026-11-11): each is a rounded body (`air::ellipsoid`) with a head, a beak and a tail where it has them, and wings, fins and tails that are pointed swept blades (`air::blade_mesh`), hinged and beaten in code. `AIR_SHOW=1` sets one of each out still by the start to be looked at. They are built here and not by the creature pipeline, which cannot yet rig a wing or six legs.
- **Fish** (`air::fish`): four schools of seven keep to water near the camera that is deep enough, circling a hand's depth under the surface with tails beating, 13 to 35 cm long; now and then one leaps and a ring spreads where it did. Dressing like the birds: nothing eats them and they cannot be caught. Fish to catch are creatures and belong to `cube_sim`.
- **Small life is true to size and kept close** (2026-11-11). For an hour butterflies and birds were drawn two to three times life size so that they showed in pictures; the developer ruled that everything in the game is its real size, small life included. What stays from that hour is where they are, not how big: butterflies (9 cm across) within 15 m of the camera, small birds (12 cm) within 26 m and flying more often, five swallows (33 cm across) looping round the camera a few metres up by day, hawks (1.2 m across) wheeling at 35 to 50 m. They are small in a still picture, as they are in life; they are seen because they move.
- **Life in the air is sparing** (2026-11-11): the developer wants life, not a snowstorm. Pollen is seventy faint specks; fireflies come out from dusk, sixty of them in clusters near the camera, each lit for a moment every few seconds, green and small; leaves come down from the real trees near the camera, one now and then and a flurry from one crown when a gust passes. A light that follows the camera's exposure must start from 'not yet set', not 0: full night is an exposure of 0, and the fireflies never lit.
- **Small birds and dragonflies** (`air::songbirds`, `dragonflies`, 2026-11-11). A small bird lives on the ground near the camera: a few hops, a peck, a look round, now and then a low arcing flight elsewhere, wings folded when down; any walking creature within 4.5 m puts it up and it goes further. A dragonfly hangs over water, darts two metres in a sixth of a second and hangs again. Movement is what makes them read as alive: each has rests and bursts, never a steady glide.
- **Water reflects the land** (2026-11-11; `--no-reflect` for the old forward path). Screen-space reflections in Bevy 0.20 want the scene rendered deferred: the default opaque method is deferred; an observer puts the depth and deferred prepasses, `ScreenSpaceReflections` and temporal anti-aliasing on every camera the moment it is made (a camera one frame without them, with deferred materials about, panics in `bevy_pbr`); the wind extension gives its prepass shader as its deferred vertex shader; the water is opaque and near mirror-smooth. Two things made the first working try show only sky: the default settings skip any surface smoother than 0.08 (the water is 0.04), and ten steps do not reach a far bank (48 do). Grain in the reflection is what the temporal anti-aliasing is for. Frame times are unchanged.
- **Water is not seen through** (2026-11-11). Blended, it wrote no depth, so the mist was measured through it to the lake's bed and every contour of the bed showed as a stripe across the water (found by rendering once with `--reflect`, where the water is opaque and had none). Shallows are paler by colour instead. And nothing at or under the water's level is put in the big blocks of rough country: rounded up to a metre, the bed stood out of a lake in bars.
- **Water has shallows, a shoreline and a murk** (2026-11-11): the mesher makes it paler and clearer where it is under a metre deep and lighter still along the very edge; with the camera under the surface the haze becomes green-blue murk a few metres deep. Not done: reflections of the land in it, waves on the sea, anything flowing.
- **Animals keep their hours** (`Mind::about`, set each tick from the niche): out of them one mostly stays resting where it is, though it still flees and fights. Those of the dusk are about a good deal by day too.
- **Water moves** (`world::ripples`, `ripple`): a tile of crossing wave trains as a normal map, four metres to a repeat, slid downwind; smooth enough to throw the sun back. At a low sun it throws back too much and whites out: not fixed.
- **Not done**: wind that changes (it is one direction and strength, written in the shaders and in `air.rs`), water that moves, sound.

### Paint

- **A photographed grain takes painted shapes over it** (`texture::atlas_with`, 2026-11-11): grass, earth, bark, moss, litter and cut wood. A photograph of grass from a hand's breadth is true and, from where a person stands, an even speckle with nothing the eye can hold (the developer: "plain ground, plain trees"); the grain made in code has the shapes (strokes of blades in clumps, clods, furrows) and none of the fineness. The photograph is multiplied by the painted grain, so the ground has both. Stone, sand, pebbles and snow stay as photographed: painted over, they were worse. `--plain` still shows the paint alone.

- **One atlas of grains, made in code at start** (`cube_voxel::texture`, 2026-11-11): fifteen tiles of 128 texels (1.5 cm) with a border of 16 that repeats the tile's far side, and four smaller copies each half the last. The border is what lets the picture be drawn smooth and shrunk with distance without a tile taking colour from its neighbour. No art files, no engine types: the game wraps the bytes in two images.
- **A grain may be a photograph** (`texture::atlas_with`, `Photo`): the game looks for `assets/textures/NAME.jpg` and `NAME_n.jpg` for each grain and hands their texels in; `cube_voxel` still reads no files. A photograph is brought to the same mean as any tile, so the block's colour tints it and far levels need nothing new. Tiles are 512 texels (4 mm) with a border of 64, six levels.
- **A grain is three things**: brightness in broad shapes and fine ones (`texel`: blades, grit, the fibre of bark), hue (`hue`: dry and lush blades, russet and gold in fallen leaves, lichen and rust on rock, moss on bark), and relief (a normal map taken from the brightness, so a low sun rakes across bark and stone). The first grain was brightness alone in broad shapes, and the developer saw no texture at all: one colour made lighter and darker reads as plastic.
- **The mesher gives every corner a place in it** from where the corner is in the world, not in its cube: tops lie flat, sides stand upright, and the tile repeats every eight cells, so bits, whole cells and the far levels of detail all show the same picture at the same place.
- **A grain multiplies the block's colour and every tile averages 0.8 in each of red, green and blue**; the mesher divides the block's colour by that. So the far levels need no other colours, and grain can be changed without touching a block.
- **Still one material and one draw a chunk.** Not done: grain that differs by biome or wetness, more than one tile to a grain (a wall repeats every two metres), roughness maps, and merging faces (the per-cube shade is small now but still in the vertices).
- **Every scene shot says what it cost** (`pace`): the middle and worst frame of the last sixty, the terrain faces in view, and what drew them. A scene has no window, and with none the loop idles at sixty a second: until 2026-11-11 every pace read 17 ms whatever the frame cost. The idle wait is now nothing in a scene, so the number is the work. Frame times compare only on the same machine; faces in view compare anywhere.

## The world

Built 2026-11-10. `cube_sim::terrain` makes it; `cargo world survey` and `map` judge it.

- **An island from a seed**, 384 m across (`SEED`, `RADIUS`). The same seed is always the same world, in the game, in tests and in `cargo world`. An island because a finite world needs an edge, and sea to the horizon is an edge nobody questions. It is a stand-in: the world is to be procedural and far larger, a small version of a real one (see Where the world goes).
- **Every size comes from one table, in metres** (`cube_sim::scale`), measured against a person of 1.75 m: the landforms (how far across, how tall, how rough), how tall each kind of tree stands, how much room it has, the classes of rock, the heights where rock and snow begin. `terrain` and `plants` carry no sizes of their own, and `cargo world survey` holds what was made to bars in the same file (the middle tree at least 8 people tall, the largest rock ten times the smallest, 65% of lowland meadow and forest easy ground). The reason: the world was scaled up three times by hand, a number at a time, and each time something was left behind and the person looked like a giant on a model. Scale is a ratio between things, so it is kept in one place and measured.
- **The height is those landforms added up** (`terrain::column`, a pure function of a point and the seed): a lowland; smooth hills over it (two layers of noise, since every layer added makes the ground as steep again, and lumps every few metres are what made it a model); one mountain and one lesser height, placed by the seed well out from the start and each a bell, so the outer third is foothills and the middle is climbing; ridges and ledges on the mountain only, the ledges wandering and missing from whole faces so they are not rings; a river in a broad valley; the shore. The first mountains were wherever a noise ran high: on this island that was most of the rim, as flat-topped walls standing straight up from the plain (seen with `cargo world grade`, which was written to find out why only a third of the lowland was easy ground).
- **Biomes come from height, slope and a moisture noise**: shore, meadow, forest, rock (high or steep), snow. A biome decides the surface block, what is planted (broadleaf trees, pines higher up, bushes, boulders) and who lives there.
- **`Land` is kept beside the voxels**: every column's height and biome, and the trees. Placing creatures and measuring the world read it, not the cells.
- **Things are their real size, drawn in cubes.** An oak stands 13 to 24 m, a birch 11 to 19, a pine 17 to 34, a willow 9 to 14, six and a half metres apart in a forest; every other measure of a tree (trunk, limbs, crown) is a part of its height. The first trees were 5 to 8 m and read as saplings; the second were 9 to 18 m and still made the person a giant.
- **A tree is a tree, not a pole with a ball on it** (`cube_sim::plants`). A trunk flares into roots at the ground and tapers upward with a lean; limbs leave it, bend and fork; foliage is separate clumps at the ends of the limbs, so sky shows through and no two trees are alike. Five kinds: oak (broad, heavy-limbed), birch (slender, pale bark, light and high), pine (whorls of drooping boughs under a point, dead stubs below the green), willow (short and thick by water, hanging in curtains), and the dead snag. Which grows where follows the land: conifers high and on rock, willows within a few metres of water, the rest mixed. The first trees were a square column and one blob, and from the ground a forest was a field of sticks.
- **What is made by the generator is shaped to the bit where a cell is too coarse.** A rock is stone with seven to ten flat faces struck off it, cut at 5 cm. Rocks come in three classes (stones underfoot 0.3 to 0.8 m, boulders 1 to 2.6 m, the rare great one 3.2 to 6 m that is a landmark) and four forms (a block, a slab lying flat, a tooth standing up, a hump mostly buried), each sat on the ground under it with smaller pieces against it and stones fallen round a large one. Built from whole cells a boulder was a pile of crates; as rounded lumps of one size they were uniform orbs. Terrain, trunks and foliage stay whole cells: a face of a broken cell is 25 squares where a whole one is one.
- **A wood has an understory.** Between the trees, on a finer grid of their own, stand young ones three to seven metres tall with their crowns at eye level, bushes from knee to head high, fallen logs and stumps. Without them everything green was overhead.
- **Ground cover is drawn as blades, not cubes, and is walked through.** Long grass, flowers, ferns and reeds are cells that are not solid. A blade of grass is two planes crossed on the diagonals of a bit (5 cm), seen from both sides, painted with the grass's grain and dark at its foot; a tuft is five to eight of them, and they grow in patches over much of a meadow. As five-sided boxes, three to a tuft, a meadow was sparse sticks; thickened as boxes it was more faces than all the land under it (2.35 million drawn from the start against a bar of 2).
- **Trees and bushes are solid blocks.** A crown starts 4.5 m up so the golem walks under it. Creatures will not climb onto leaves; the player can.
- **Judged by numbers first** (`cargo world survey`): water 20% to 60%, each of meadow, forest and rock at least 6%, 400 trees, ground at least 40 m high, cliffs on 3% to 35% of the land, half the land reached from the start on foot and nine tenths by swimming, at most 2 million faces drawn from the start, and the scale bars above. `cargo world grade` says where the ground is easy, steep and cliff by height, and draws it. A seed that fails one is not a world to ship; seed 6 passes and is the default.
- **Not yet:** caves and overhangs (the land is a height map), shaped blocks, textures.
- **Where the world goes** (decided 2026-11-10): procedural and much larger, a small version of a real world, not one island held whole in memory. What that needs, in order: `Land` answering for any column from the seed instead of holding an array of all of them; cells made a chunk at a time near the player and dropped behind, with what was changed kept; trees and rocks placed by a rule that gives the same answer whichever chunk asks first (a tree's crown crosses chunks); where mountains, plains, forests and seas are coming from slow maps (height of the land, warmth, wet) instead of one placed mountain; creatures placed and put away as the player moves; and beyond the last chunks one coarse mesh from the height map. The scale table is what such a world is generated from, and `column` is already a pure function of a point.

- **What the ground is at a place comes from what is there** (`terrain::fill`, 2026-11-11), not from its biome alone:
  - **Trails** (`terrain::worn`, `Land::trail`): the level lines of a slow bent noise, so they wander and fork and one passes within a hundred metres of anywhere; a metre of bare earth, trodden grass each side, long grass along the edge. Nothing stands in one (`Land::thing`), which is what makes it a way through a wood. Only on meadow and forest floor that is not steep. They are not yet roads between places. The larger animals go by them (`mind::think`): of the first places one thinks of roaming to, it takes only one that lies on a trail, so `cargo world run` finds about one in six standing on a trail at any moment where trails are a fiftieth of the ground.
  - **Under a tree is that tree's ground**: `fill` gathers the trees whose crowns are over a region, and a column knows how far under a crown it is. Bare earth at the foot of a trunk; fallen leaf in drifts thickening toward it (a bed of needles under a pine), under a lone meadow oak as much as in a wood; in its shade the grass thins and ferns and mushrooms come up.
  - **Forest floor**: turf, leaf drifts, moss where it is dampest, ferns in colonies, mushrooms in troops on the litter. **Meadow**: flowers in drifts some metres over, long grass in its own patches, scrapes of bare earth, and the grass itself dry and straw-coloured in stretches and lush in others (`mesh::sward`). **Shore**: beds of pebbles in the sand, reeds in stands at the water, marram on the dry sand behind. **Rock**: grass in the cracks of low rock.
  - `make floors` pictures a trail, a forest floor, a meadow and a shore in one sheet; `cargo world find trail` says where an open stretch of trail is.

## What is placed is data

The developer's rule (2026-11-11): what varies is data, and a new kind of anything that is placed in the world by rule is added without touching the code that places it. Where this stands:

- **Creatures**: `assets/creatures/ID/creature.toml` (`[life]`: temper, habitat, water, cover, hours, numbers, voice; `[built]` for those forge makes).
- **Trees**: `assets/world/flora.toml` (`cube_sim::flora`). A kind is its name, its habit (one of five shapes, the only part in code: `plants::tree`), its height, girth, spread and reach, its bark and leaf, and how common it is on low ground, high ground and by water. Beech, aspen and spruce were added as data alone. The land picks by site from the table (`flora::pick`), the far stand-ins and the ground under a crown ask the kind what it is.
- **The ground and what grows on it**: `assets/world/land.toml` (`cube_sim::cover`). How thick trees, undergrowth and rocks stand in each biome; what the undergrowth is, by shares; and two lists of rules, for the top of the soil and for the cell over it. A rule is tests of a place (how worn by a trail, how far under a crown and whether a conifer's, how damp, what it stands on, wet or dry, slope, height, a patch of slow noise, a chance) and what to put where they all hold; the first that fits is taken. `terrain::fill` asks and places. Woodland flowers in the glades were added as one rule and no code.
- **Not yet data**: which biome a place is (six, by height, slope and damp, in `terrain::biome_of`; a seventh would want the `Biome` enum to become a table too, and it is named in some two hundred places); the sizes and lines in `scale`; rocks' classes there; the small life of the air (niches in `ecology`, numbers in `air`).

## What lives in it

- **Numbers are made by the island, not set** (`Sim::day`, 2026-11-11; the developer: "if the simulation isn't like real life we have failed. That means doing more than just putting a few creatures here"). The creatures near the player live second by second; the rest, a thousand and more, live by the day: the old die, what eats meat must find prey within its range or starve, what eats plants dies for want of food where it outnumbers what its ground feeds, a few die of the winter, pairs have young on the first day of spring (fewer the fuller the ground), and a group grown to twice its size sends half to find ground of their own. Nothing dead is put back: until this day the dead returned where they had lived after two and a half minutes.
  - What a kind eats, its litter, its years and what its ground feeds are data (`[life]`: `diet`, `litter`, `lifespan`, `capacity`, `share`), and unsaid they follow from its size: the small breed fast and die young. A hunter ranges two and a half times as far as what it hunts and keeps a territory that holds one group; a pack shares a large kill. A thing of stone or one not alive (`diet = "none"`) neither eats, breeds nor ages.
  - **`cargo world years N`** runs the island by the day for N years and prints each kind's numbers year by year, with what was born and what died of what; it fails if a kind that began as six or more dies out or one grows past five times its start. Its first run killed everything in a year: the island had been stocked with a hunter to every two of the hunted (84 wolves, 83 goblins). Hunters are now a tenth to a half as common (`share`), and eight years hold: rabbits and squirrels rise to what their ground feeds, deer and ponies fall a third under the wolves and steady, wolves number four to nine.
  - The young are born small and grow (`Sim::grown`, drawn by `motion`).
  - **What is awake lives by the same needs** (`mind`, `Sim::step`): it grows hungry and thirsty by the second at the day's rates. Thirsty, it walks to the nearest water within sixty metres and drinks. What eats plants grazes because it is hungry, little when full and nearly always when empty; two hours' grazing is a meal. What hunts hunts from hunger: fed, it lets a rabbit go by, and a person too, unless they come within a few steps (what is hostile needs no reason); a kill feeds it for days. `cargo world run` says how often each kind drank.
  - **What is awake starves as what is out of sight does** (2026-11-11): a meat-eater near the player hungers by the second; past three meals missed it loses strength, and at four (`life::STARVES`, the number `day` uses) it lies down and dies where it is. Until then an awake hunter was hungered twice (by the second and by the day) and could not die of it.
  - **Not yet**: plants are not eaten down or regrown, so nothing that grazes can starve in sight; nothing sleeps in a den or keeps a nest.

- **A kind keeps its own ground** (`Sim::populate`): no group settles within 80 m of another of its kind (30 m for those that live alone). Herds went wherever suited them and three of ponies stood within a stone's throw: one cluster, to the developer. The pony is 1.6 m tall, not 1.45.

Built 2026-11-10. `cube_sim::life` and `mind`; `cargo world run` judges it.

- **The simulation owns every creature.** `Sim` holds an agent for each: its body, who drives it (the keys, its own mind, nobody) and what it is doing (`Act`, which is a clip's name). The game keeps a model in step with each agent and never moves one itself. So the same tick runs in the window, in a test and in `cargo world run`, and a server later.
- **A mind is a temper and a handful of states**, not a behaviour tree. Tempers, set in `creature.toml` `[life]`: *timid* (rabbit, deer) grazes and runs from people, hunters and monsters; *hunter* (wolf) runs timid creatures down, then is spent for a while; *wary* (bear, boar) faces whatever comes within 7 m and hits it if it comes closer; *hostile* (goblin, troll) goes for people on sight. A deer bolts from a wolf at 12 m and lets a bear come to 5. Enough for a world that looks alive and for the player to have something to run from; more is added when a need (hunger, sleep) asks for it.
- **No path planning.** A creature heads for a point and looks a step ahead: it turns aside from walls, drops and water it would have to swim, trying ways further round until one is open, and its own box must be able to move that way. At its ease it takes half-metre steps and drops of a metre, so it stays on the ground it knows; fleeing or chasing it jumps a metre and drops three. Good enough on open ground; a creature can still be walled in, and then it waits.
- **Roaming keeps to a home**: a group shares one, placed in the kind's biome. A creature that flees settles where it stops.
- **Nothing dies yet.** A blow is a shove and a flinch (`hurt`). Health, death and what the player hits back with come with combat.
- **A swimmer climbs out over a bank** up to half a metre above its waist. Without it a steep-sided river was a trap, for the player too; the headless run found it.
- **Random choices come from one seeded generator** in the `Sim`, and agents are visited in id order, so a seed replays exactly (tested).
- **Judged by numbers** (`cargo world run`): no creature ends a tick inside the ground or falls out of the world, each kind walks into something for at most 8% of the time, is walled in for at most 10%, swims for at most 5%, and goes at least 3 m.

## Fighting, and what a person carries

Built 2026-11-11, in the simulation (`cube_sim::life`, `items`) and tested there; the game shows it (`cube_game::hud`).

- **Health is by size**: a rabbit 22, a wolf 56, a bear 91, a golem 345; a person 100. A blow takes what the striker's size gives (4 + 9 a metre of height), or for a person what is in hand: fists 6, a torch 9, a shovel 12, a short sword 28.
- **A blow lands a third of a second into the swing** on the nearest thing within a metre and a half in front. Minds time their own; a driven creature asks with `Sim::swing`.
- **The dead lie, are gone, and return.** At no health a creature falls (`Act::Die`), lies 25 s, and is removed; 150 s later another of its kind comes to live where it did, so hunting and being hunted do not empty the world. A person wakes at home after five seconds, whole. Left in peace eight seconds, anything mends two a second.
- **A pack is 24 slots, the first eight a bar to hand, one of those in the hand** (`items::Pack`). A person sets out with a short sword, a torch, a shovel and six berries. `Sim::use_held` is the one way a thing is used: a weapon swings, berries are eaten for 15 health (only by the hurt), the shovel asks the game to dig. The torch is lit while it is the thing in hand.
- **Keys**: left button uses what is in hand; 1 to 8 take a slot, Q and E step along the bar, T takes the torch; I opens the pack (click a slot, then another, to change them over); F1 opens the admin panel (time, season and speed; heal, hurt, give, go to water or wood or meadow or rock; put any creature down ahead; nothing hurts me). In a script: `use`, `torch`, `hold3`.
- **`make fight`** runs it all in the real game with no window: a script puts a goblin down in front of the player (`spawn=goblin`) and swings; the trace must show the player hurt, the goblin killed, and the swing and the goblin heard.
- **Tested without a window**: the rules in `cube_sim` tests; from the game, a scripted `--play --scene` prints `health=`, `held=` and `kills=` in its trace. The bar, the pack and the admin panel are drawn into a scene with `--hud bar|pack|admin`, and two of them are in `make slice`.
- **A kill gives meat** to a killer who carries a pack, by the size of what was killed; eaten, it mends 35. **A guard** (the right button held, with anything but the shovel in hand) turns seven tenths of a blow that comes from in front.
- **A bow** is in the pack from the start: its blow reaches thirty metres in a line five degrees either side, where a blade's reaches a step and sweeps wide (`Item::reach`); it takes 16. No arrow is drawn or flies: what is in line when the shot lands is hit.
- **An arrow is seen** to fly from the archer when the bow is loosed (`player::arrows`); what it hit was already decided by the simulation.
- **The player's state is saved** (`Sim::save`, `load`, tested): when it is, where they are, their health and pack, as a few lines of text in `terra-cube.save` where the game is run from. F5 writes it, F9 reads it, `--continue` starts from it; in a script, `save` and `load`. The land is the seed's and is not written, so what was dug or built is lost, as are the creatures' whereabouts.
- **Armour**: a leather jerkin, while it is anywhere in the pack, takes a quarter off every blow (the admin panel gives one; none is found in the world yet).
- **Not done**: hit shapes per limb, anything lying on the ground to be picked up, saving what was dug.

## Sound

- **The air is quiet beds and single small sounds, not loops** (2026-11-11). The developer, after the first round: "some of the sounds are still ambiently too loud or too simple and repetitive". Measured before changing anything, a five-minute stand in the noon meadow gave three things: an eighteen-second loop of birdsong going round, the hawks overhead calling every few seconds (each bird had its own timer, and there are a dozen), and a herd of horses neighing one recording seventeen times. So, by design and not by one slider:
  - *Loudness.* `Mix::air` is 0.3 (was 0.45), and the beds' own weights are wind 0.3 (swelling and falling between 0.35 and 1 of that by `gust`, three slow sines that never fall into step), the night's insects 0.14, water 0.22. With the files levelled to -27 dB that puts a meadow's wind between -62 and -53 dB, a summer night's bed at -56, a river at its bank at -53, against the walker's own footfall at -42 (mean over the file; its loudest fifth of a second -40). The small sounds are set so the nearest bird (never under 18 m) peaks under a footfall. The reason for these numbers and no lower: the air is still to be heard when standing still, and the admin slider goes to 1.5 for an ear to settle it.
  - *Repetition.* No bed is one file: each is a set (`amb_wind_1`..`_6`, 176 s of three recordings; `amb_water_1`..`_4`, 114 s of two) of which one plays, and every 24 to 54 s another comes in under it over seven seconds at a speed a little its own (0.94 to 1.06) while the first goes out. A bed that is not heard here is not played. The looped birdsong is gone: birds are single calls, a phrase of one to four from one perch (a real songbird's when one is out and near, so the one seen is the one heard), of a kind that suits how wooded it is (`SINGERS`: meadowlark and sparrow in the open, wren and chickadees between, warbler, nuthatch and thrush in the wood), ten kinds with three to seven calls each. No set plays the same file twice running (`Bank::pick`), and a set of one file is thrown further off its pitch (12%, not 7).
  - *Small sounds*, each seldom, from somewhere about, and how often is `ecology::ambience`'s to say (new fields `leaves`, `insects`, `frogs`, `owls`, tested): a raven, crow, woodpecker's drum or dove from 45 to 95 m; the hawks, one call between them every minute or two; leaves when the wind gusts, where there are trees; grasshoppers, a cicada and a bee low in the grass on a summer's day in the open; frogs within reach of water from dusk; an owl in the wood at night; and the night's crickets one by one, up to five, each at its own place, pace and pitch, chirping in bouts.
  - *They are placed by our own rule, not the engine's.* The engine's sound falls with the square of the distance past 5 m, which has a bird at 50 m unheard; the small sounds are played from 4 m off in their direction (so they are heard from that side) at a loudness that halves with each doubling of distance past 12 m (`Bank::far`).
  - *Creatures' calls have a budget.* No call within six seconds of another's, a kind silent for a minute after one of it has called, and the kind that called last waits for another kind or three minutes: a herd is many animals with one voice.
  - *Judged by measurement* (`make sound`, in `make prove`): `tools/audio/listen.sh PLACE HOUR [DAY]` stands in a place for five minutes of the real game with its clock run six times as fast (`--speed 6`; five minutes of the game are five hours of its day), and `tools/audio/mix.py` reads the trace (every sound now says how loud it reached the ear and when, and each bed says so once a second) with every file's own level: how many different files, the longest run of one file, the air's mean and loudest fifth of a second against a footfall in the same run, and how much of the time a small sound is sounding. Bars: mean 6 dB under a footfall, peak no more than 3 over one, no file more than twice running, eight files. Meadow in summer from ten in the morning: 28 small files in 49 plays and 6 beds, longest run 1, air -53 dB against a footfall at -42, a small sound 18% of the time (most of it grasshoppers and a cicada; 7% before they were added). Shore from six in the evening: -50 dB, 50% of the time (the crickets). Runs differ by a decibel or two: what plays depends on the frame.
  - *Where they are from*: the sound libraries of Yellowstone and Rocky Mountain National Parks and Wikimedia Commons, public domain or CC0 only. The calls were cut from the recordings by `tools/audio/cut.py`, which finds what stands over the quiet of a recording; `tools/audio/cut.sh` holds every cut so it can be made again. OpenGameArt refuses automated fetching and was left alone.
  - *Not done, and not known.* Nobody has listened. The night's bed is still one eleven-second recording (no other free of conditions was found), under made crickets (`make.sh`: tone pulses), which may sound made. The cuts were chosen by level: one may hold two birds, or half a phrase. The birds are North American and European kinds together. Sets still of one or two files that are heard often: `swim` (1), `splash` (2), `swing`, `hit`, `hurt` (2 each), and most animals' voices (a horse, a deer and a boar have one recording each).

- **Every kind has a sound of its own** (2026-11-11). Eight had another's (fox and lynx the wolf's, moose the deer's, ogre the troll's, badger the boar's, squirrel and hedgehog the rabbit's, the giant toad the frog's). Moose, hedgehog, toad and ogre now have recordings from Commons (CC0 and public domain); for fox, lynx, badger and squirrel nothing free of conditions was found there, and they have voiced imitations from a CC0 pack, chosen by measured length and pitch. Where a recording is still of an animal of another size (the giant toad's is a hand-long toad's) it is pitched to the animal: (recorded height / its own)^0.35, between 0.7 and 1.5. Each is played at (recorded animal's height / its own)^0.35, held between 0.7 and 1.5, so a fox is a fifth higher than a wolf and a moose a tenth lower than a deer. Beaver, crab, lizard and tortoise, which had none, have sounds made here (`tools/audio/make.sh`: a tail on the water, claws, a hiss, a grunt), as has the dragonfly's wing; the hawks overhead have a hawk's call (CC0, Commons) where they had a crow's; swallows call as the small birds do, and a leaping fish is heard to splash. A butterfly is silent, as it is. Not done: recordings of their own for those that share, and nobody has listened to the made ones.
- **Mixed by ear, in the admin panel** (`audio::Mix`, F1: air, creatures' voices, footfalls). Nothing here can listen; the developer did, and found the birds in his face, the ambience and footfalls bad and the river like a drain. What was done without ears: all three kinds turned down by default; a bird's call is never played from nearer than twelve metres and comes less often; running water is heard within nine metres of it, not twenty-two. The water loop and the footfalls were then replaced by recordings of the thing itself (a shallow stony river, public domain; steps on soft ground and on stone, CC0): the first water loop came from a pack of splash effects and the first steps from a pack of made sounds, which is what the ear had said. `tools/audio/steady.py` measures how even a loop is and how much of it is low rumble; the wind was all under 300 Hz, a rumble, and now has a band of made noise over it (300 to 1700 Hz, swelling in gusts that fit the loop), so 15% of it is low. None of this has been listened to since.

Built 2026-11-11 (`cube_game::audio`); the files are CC0, in `assets/audio`, whose README gives each one's source.

- **A sound is a set of files found by name**: `wolf` is `wolf_1.ogg` and `wolf_2.ogg`, one is picked and played a little off its pitch. A creature's voice is the name of a set, in `[life] voice` of its `creature.toml`.
- **The air of a place is the ecology's** (`ecology::ambience`, tested): wind by how open and high it is, birdsong by day where there are trees and little in winter, insects on warm nights, water by nearness to it. (At first four loops played always, their loudness eased to it; see the first entry for what replaced them.)
- **A creature is heard when the simulation says it does something**: its voice with a blow, a hit when wounded, a death; and its call now and then at the hours its niche says it is about. Heard from where it is, to 45 m. The player's feet sound for each stride by what is underfoot (grass, stone, shallows), with a splash going in and a stroke for each while swimming.
- **The small birds and the ones overhead are heard from where they are** (`air::Sings`, `audio::singers`): each now and then, only while it is out.
- **Checked without listening**: every sound played is a line of the trace (`sound step_grass_2 vol=0.58 at 9,12,6`), so a scripted `--play --scene` run can be read for what was heard. `--quiet` for none.
- **Levels are measured, not heard** (`tools/audio/level.py`): every one-shot is brought to a mean of -20 dB and every loop to -27 dB, no peak over -1 dB; 37 of the 52 files were out, by up to 7 dB. Run it after adding a file.
- **Loops join their own start, by measurement** (`tools/audio/loops.py`): the jump from a loop's last sample to its first must be no more than is usual between neighbouring samples inside it; one of the six failed and had its last second faded into its first.
- **Not known:** how any of it sounds. The files were chosen by title and length by an agent that cannot hear, levels are not matched, and several loops' seams are unheard. A person must listen and prune. No fox bark or true wolf howl was to be had under CC0.

## Ecology

Built 2026-11-11. One system decides what life a place has (`cube_sim::ecology`); `cargo world life` judges it.

- **A place is described once, for everything that might live there** (`Place`): its biome, metres to the nearest water, how deep the water is, tree cover from open sky to deep wood (the trees within eight metres), height over the sea. `Ecology::new` measures the island for it every two metres.
- **A kind of life has a niche** (`Niche`): the biomes it lives in and how well each suits it, how near water it keeps (`edge`, `near`, `away`, `in`), how much cover it wants (`open`, `edge`, `deep`), and when it is about (`day`, `night`, `dusk`). `Niche::fit(place, hour)` is 0 to 1.
- **Everything that puts life in the world asks it.** Animals: `[life]` in `creature.toml` carries `habitat`, `water`, `cover` and `hours`, and `Sim::populate` settles a group where its niche fits, the rare kinds first. Small life the game draws: butterflies, songbirds, swallows, dragonflies, fireflies, fish, falling leaves each have a niche in `ecology.rs`, and `air.rs` places every one through `settles`. A firefly is in damp woodland within reach of water from dusk; near the camera only if the camera is somewhere like that. Before, each kind had its own rule of the form "near the camera, on any dry ground".
- **Why in the simulation:** where things live is a rule of the world, and the same answer must hold for a deer that can be hunted and a butterfly that is only seen.
- **Winter thins what is about** (`Niche::cold`, an animal's `winter` in `[life]`): in the depth of it there are no butterflies, dragonflies, fireflies or swallows, fewer songbirds, and the bear, hedgehog, frog, toad, tortoise and lizard mostly lie up. By early spring all are back.
- **Not done:** nothing eats or drinks, so a deer keeps near water only because it was put there; no seasons or weather; the ogre has no home on this island (it wants level snow, and there is almost none since the mountain moved north).

## A big world

Built 2026-11-11: the island is a kilometre across (`terrain::RADIUS`), and only the part round the player is held in cells.

- **The lie of the land is known for all of it, always** (`Land::new`, a second or two on every core): each column's height and biome, and, decided from the land alone, where every tree stands, of what kind and how tall, and how big each rock is. So the ecology, the far land and the tools can speak of the whole island though almost none of it is made.
- **Cells are made a region at a time** (`terrain::fill`; a region is 32 m square): the ground, what stands on it, the ground cover. The same call makes the same cells whenever and in whatever order it is asked, which is what lets a region be dropped and made again. A tree whose crown reaches in from outside is built with the region, so no crown is cut at an edge.
- **The game makes regions within 176 m of the camera and drops them beyond 240 m** (`world::regions`), nearest first, a few milliseconds a frame; a region someone has dug or built in is kept, since nothing is saved yet. About 1.3 GB held, as the whole of the old 384 m island was.
- **Beyond the cells, the far land** (`cube_game::far`): the whole island as a light mesh from the height map, a region to a piece and four metres between points, with a plain shape of the right kind and height for each tree and a sheet of water over what is wet. A piece is hidden when its region's real ground is there and near. A mountain two valleys off is there to be walked to.
- **Creatures are lived only near the watcher** (`Sim::focus`, `life::AWAKE` = 110 m): the island has some two thousand, a couple of hundred awake at a time; the rest are as they were left, and are stood clear of whatever is there when they wake. The game gives a model only to the ones awake (`motion::mirror`).
- **A bigger island has more mountains**: two near the start as before, and two more for every 384 m square of island, placed and sized by the seed.
- **The tools judge what the game has**: `cargo world` makes the whole island's land and the cells within 160 m of the start, and lives the creatures near a person there.
- **Not done**: saving (so dropped regions forget nothing only because edited ones are kept); the far land has no rocks or undergrowth and its trees are plain shapes; the land itself is still one island held as one array (64 MB), not made as it is come to: ten kilometres wants `Land` in pieces too.

## Seeing far

Started 2026-11-10, when the island was drawn whole at full detail and hazed out from 80 m.

- **Three levels of detail a chunk, chosen by distance** (`world::LEVELS`): every cell to 40 m; cells taken two a side to 104 m (a quarter of the faces); four a side beyond (a sixteenth). `mesh_chunk_far` makes the coarse ones: a group of cells is solid if any is, and is the block highest in it, so distant ground has no holes and trees keep their bulk; ground cover and the bits of broken cells are left out.
- **Bevy's `VisibilityRange` does the switching**, with a fade of 8 m between levels so nothing pops. Every level of a chunk is drawn from an entity at the chunk's middle, with its vertices given from there: the fade is computed from the entity's origin, and with all chunks at the world's origin every one faded as if it stood there (the first try: half the island ghosted, the rest missing). `use_aabb` is not for crossfades.
- **Haze from 180 m to 700 m**, so the island is clear from end to end and the sea has no edge.
- **A chunk's level is meshed when the camera first comes near enough to need it** (`world::stream`), nearest first, in a slice of each frame (4 ms; 50 ms for the first three seconds and for scenes), and dropped when far behind. The island doubled in width on that. Still to do, in order:
  1. Meshing off the main thread.
  2. Merge flat faces of one colour (greedy meshing). The per-cell tint has to move out of the vertices first (a small noise texture or a shader), or nothing merges.
  3. Make and keep chunks only near the player, the rest as the seed and what has been changed: then the world is as big as wanted.
  4. Beyond the last chunks, one coarse mesh drawn from `Land`'s height map and biome colours, to the horizon: mountains seen from kilometres off cost almost nothing.
- **Judged by** `cargo world survey`: the faces drawn standing at the start, each chunk at the level its distance gives it (bar: 2 million).

## Creatures

Full detail in [pipeline.md](pipeline.md). The design choices:

- **Creatures are stylised low-poly skinned meshes, not voxel models.** Eco does the same. The world is blocks; what lives in it is not. Tried the other way on 2026-11-10 and decided against: creatures rebuilt from the world's 5 cm cubes (`cargo forge cubes`) can look right standing still when the model is drawn for it, but took a dozen rounds to get one figure there, come apart at the joints in motion, lose small animals altogether, and would mean buying every creature again. A creature should belong to the world by its style (chunky shapes, flat colour, a shared palette), not by being made of the same cubes. The trial, its checks and what it showed are kept in [pipeline.md](pipeline.md#cubes). Three other looks were then drawn and two built beside the house style (carved from blocks, flat-shaded, hand-painted; [pipeline.md](pipeline.md#trying-a-look)), and the house style was kept: faceted, bulky, flat colour.
- **Tripo makes mesh, texture, skeleton and skin weights. We make most of the motion.** Tripo has a dozen preset clips for bipeds and exactly one (a walk) for anything else, so animals' other clips are made in code against bone *roles* read from the skeleton's shape. One recipe serves every species; Spore did the same.
- **Clips are baked into the model file by forge, not computed in the game.** A baked clip can be checked by numbers and seen on a sheet before it ships, and the game just plays glTF. The same Rust recipes can move into the game later for what must react at runtime (feet on slopes, looking at things).
- **One skeleton for every biped** (Tripo's Mixamo-named rig, 65 joints). The player and humanoid monsters share bone names, so clips and gear are shared.
- **Player customisation, in order of cost** (none built yet): tint and texture swaps on the one base body; rigid gear (helmet, pauldrons, weapons) parented to a single bone, which needs no skin weights and so suits generated meshes; body-part swaps on the shared skeleton only if those are not enough. Generated *skinned* clothing is avoided: fitting it to a body is the hard problem terra spent weeks on.

## Moving through the world

- **The model comes up a block's step, it is not put on it** (`motion::mirror`): where the ground is drawn in blocks, feet (and the eyes that ride on them) ease to each new level over a tenth of a second, never more than 35 cm behind. Put straight on each block as it was reached, walking was a string of jolts.

Built 2026-11-10. The rules are `cube_sim::body`; the game only feeds it keys and draws the result.

- **Our own box-against-voxels movement, not a physics engine.** A body is an upright box moved one axis at a time and put back against the cell face it crossed. A block world needs nothing more, it is about 150 lines, it runs headless, and it will behave the same on a server. A physics engine (Avian, Rapier) would bring its own world to keep in step with ours and is not deterministic across machines by default.
- **A ledge of half a metre is walked up, and down, without leaving the ground**: two cells. Every hill is a staircase of cells; needing a jump for each would be unplayable. The model eases after the body over a step so it does not pop.
- **Fixed tick, drawn in between.** Bodies step 60 times a second whatever the frame rate; the game draws them between the last two ticks.
- **Third person, camera-relative.** The models are the point of the game, so the player sees theirs. WASD is relative to the camera, the character turns to face where it goes, and ground between camera and character pulls the camera in (a ray through the voxels).
- **Every moving creature has the same body.** The keys drive one; a wander drives the rest; the player is not special. So an animal steps, falls and swims by the same rules, and any creature can be controlled (`--as wolf`).
- **Creatures do not pass through each other.** Each tick (`Sim::step`), before bodies step, overlapping ones are pushed apart over the ground (`body::separate`): two movers share the push, one standing still gives none. A creature is met as a capsule sized from its measured standing width and length (`meta.toml`): round for an upright figure, a fattened line along the body for an animal, so a wolf is bumped at its nose and not only its middle. Rounded, so they slide past instead of locking corners. `--bodies`, or the panel's tick box, draws the shapes. Against the voxel world a body is still a square box of the capsule's radius, so a long animal's head can poke into a wall.
- **The gait is asked for, and moves at its clip's speed.** Walk, run or sprint is the driver's choice (Ctrl, nothing, Shift) and the body moves at the speed forge built or measured that clip for, so feet hold: 1.2, 4.0 and 5.6 m/s for the player. While speeding up or slowing the clip's rate follows the body's speed.
- **In the air and in water the body picks the clip:** `jump` on the way up from a jump, `fall` otherwise, `land` after a drop of more than about half a metre if it is not running on, `swim` when the water is over its middle. All are made by forge ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md)).
- **Feel, in numbers** (`Tuning`): a run is reached in under a fifth of a second (18 m/s²) and stopped in an eighth (26); gravity 22 m/s²; a held jump clears a metre and a half and a tapped one is a hop of a third of that; a body turns to face the way it is going at ten radians a second, not in one tick; a jump pressed up to 0.12 s before landing still happens, and up to 0.1 s after walking off an edge; falls are capped at 25 m/s, and a body that ends a tick inside the floor is put back on top of it. The model leans a few degrees into turns and into speeding up.
- **Water:** a body floats with the surface at its middle, swims up with the jump key and down with Ctrl. Water is drawn as its own see-through mesh.
- **First person is the same camera at the eyes** with the model hidden and turned to face where the camera looks (V). Third person stays the default.
- **Feet are not placed on steps.** A body eases over a step in about a fifth of a second and its feet do not seek the riser. Other block games do the same; leg IK against voxels in the game is real work for little gain while every hill is a staircase. Shaped blocks (slopes) change that sum, so it is decided again then.
- **Not yet:** arms or body visible in first person, a camera and tint for under water, turning on the spot (the model only turns as it moves, or with the camera in first person).

## The sandbox and agent tooling

- **A script's times are on the picture's clock** (`shot::Stage::ready_for`, 2026-11-11): seconds since the scene was ready to be pictured, not since the program started. As the world grew (more ground, trees as cubes, a hundred models in the air) it took longer to be ready than a script took to run, so every `--strip` and `--gif` was of someone standing still after the script was over, and an hour went on a walk animation that was not broken. `ANIM_TRACE=player` (with any `--scene --play`) prints what a creature's clip is doing.

- `cargo world` judges the world and its creatures with no window, in under two seconds: `survey` (the land), `run` (five minutes of life, a line a kind, then `ok` or `FAIL` lines), `map` (one picture from above with where everything went), `who` (where each group lives). `WORLD_TRACE=deer cargo world run` follows the deer that ends up most stuck, a line a second, with the ground round it when it is caught. This is where behaviour and terrain are changed and checked; the window is for how it looks.
- `cargo sandbox` is the debug level: spawn any creature, play any clip, scrub, wander, give it its own mind, show skeletons. `--wild` starts the living world instead.
- `--scene FILE --wild --at X,Z` pictures any place on the island (`--view high` from the air), so a herd or a hillside can be looked at without walking there.
- `cargo sandbox --shot ID` draws offscreen to a PNG with no window, so an agent on a server can look. A sheet is one image: rows are clips, columns are moments.
- `--scene FILE --play --script '…' --strip 10` runs a scripted route with no window and saves it as a strip of pictures: how movement is judged without a screen. `--trace` prints the body.
- `--gif 12` saves the same run as a moving picture. A strip is for the agent, which reads numbers and one still; a GIF is for the person, who can see in a second whether a change of clip pops or a gait feels wrong, and the agent does not have to look at it.
- The real window runs on a machine with no GPU screen through the software Vulkan driver, and `tools/drive/drive.py` presses keys and moves the mouse in it. That is how the keyboard, mouse look and cursor lock were tested ([development.md](development.md)).
- The window opens on a loading screen when someone is to play in it (`cube_game::loading`), because the world is made a region at a time and the first seconds were spent standing in half of one. It lifts when everything within 60 m of the camera is made (the regions' cells, the chunks' meshes, the regions' trees, the models of the player and of what is awake), and its bar is those counts. Behind it the world is held still rather than hidden: the keys do nothing, the simulation only wakes what is near (`Sim::wake`: no time passes, nothing moves or is hurt) and the sound is turned down to nothing, so nobody is bitten before they can see. While it is up nearly the whole frame goes to making the world (0.12 s each to regions and to meshes; a bar needs only a few frames a second). Twenty seconds with nothing new made and it lifts anyway and says why: a model that fails to load must not be a game that never starts. It is not in a `--scene`, which does its own waiting. It prints `loading done t= regions= meshes= barred=` once, and `make window` judges it: a W pressed while it is up is counted as turned away and moves nobody.
- Wanted next: a remote port (Bevy Remote Protocol) so an agent can drive a running sandbox.
